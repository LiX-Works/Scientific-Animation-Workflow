# Physics Checklist for Editing the Film

These are the scientific constraints of the explanation. They guide source and visual review; the checklist is not certification of a fabricated device or completed independent scientific validation. Read the [scientific scope](scientific-scope.md) before changing the comparison.

| Check | Required interpretation | What to inspect |
|---|---|---|
| Electron supply | Electrons arrive from semiconductor/contact regions, not through the gate dielectric in the ideal field-effect picture | Particle paths and dielectric label |
| Depletion and inversion | In the p-type MOSFET reference, holes recede near the surface and an electron inversion layer develops above threshold | Carrier distribution and timing |
| Current direction | For the illustrated n-channel bias, electrons move S → D and conventional current points D → S | Arrows, labels and particles |
| Two electrical roles | The gate controls carrier distribution; source–drain bias drives lateral transport | Keep vertical and horizontal cues distinct |
| EDL charge signs | Anions near the positive gate; cations on the electrolyte side near the semiconductor; electrons across that interface | Legend, signs and locations |
| Both interfaces | Gate–electrolyte and semiconductor–electrolyte charge layers contribute; the bulk is approximately neutral | Macro distribution and potential profile |
| Effective distance | The EDL separation is not the full electrolyte thickness | Brackets, scale cues and text |
| Comparison quantity | Both drawings reach the same target charge change, not equal measured current or all equal properties | Shared charge parameter and voltage indicators |
| Capacitance relation | ΔQ ≈ C_eff ΔV_eff is a qualified capacitive relation | Do not treat it as a full nonlinear device solution |
| Numerical examples | The 100-fold example and bar heights are schematic, not measured or scale-faithful | Keep illustrative labels |
| Breakdown image | Defects and a connected path illustrate one gate-related failure idea | Do not add predicted lifetime, probability or threshold |
| Local field | Low external voltage does not imply weak local EDL field | Retain the limitation |
| Material strength | EDL formation does not inherently raise intrinsic breakdown strength | Avoid stronger-material or failure-proof wording |
| Electrochemistry | Finite stability regime; redox, leakage and ion insertion are separate concerns | Retain stability-window qualification |
| Lateral failure | Efficient gating does not eliminate source–drain high-field or heating problems | Preserve scope of the conclusion |
| Geometry | Particles, cutaways, grid and dimensions serve explanation | Do not call them measurements or solved ion dynamics |

Retain the conditional conclusion: **a higher effective gate capacitance can reduce the gate voltage required for a given charge-control task and may reduce risks associated with reliance on high gate voltage.**

When altering subtitles, regenerate the timing map and inspect the limitations as well as the main comparison. A correct source label is insufficient if it disappears too quickly to read in the film.
