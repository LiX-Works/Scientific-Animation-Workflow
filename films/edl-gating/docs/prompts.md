# Selected Briefs and Review Prompts

These excerpts show how the contributor directed the work. They are **edited English translations of selected passages from real Chinese production messages**, not complete transcripts or verbatim English originals. Headings and explanatory notes were added for this case study. Omissions keep the examples focused; the full private conversation is not required to run the project.

These excerpts describe the **original Chinese production brief and v5 review**. The current v6 film is the 85-second English revision; historical requests for Chinese text below are preserved as quotations, not current rendering instructions. A request in a prompt alone does not establish that every suggested effect or tool was used.

## 1. Establish the scientific task

**Source:** the contributor's detailed physics brief, later supplied in full to repair a truncated export.

> The gate voltage establishes and adjusts the conducting channel; the source–drain voltage drives carriers along it. Electric double-layer gating mainly changes the efficiency of the first process.

> A smaller effective charge-separation distance can give a large interfacial capacitance. The relevant distance is not the full thickness of the electrolyte.

> The electric double layer does not directly make the material harder to break down. It can achieve the required charge control at a lower operating gate voltage, potentially reducing related high-voltage failure risks.

**What this contributed:** a causal chain, a distinction between gate control and lateral current, and explicit limits on the breakdown claim. [Scientific scope](scientific-scope.md) carries these qualifications into the case.

## 2. Restart with a visual direction

**Source:** the contributor's new-film director brief, later supplied in full.

> Treat this as a new work. Do not inherit the previous film's structure, rhythm, wording or visual template.

> This explains physics already taught in class. Do not present it as my invention.

> Let the image be bold and the language restrained. Let the animation be striking and the physical explanation accurate.

The last two lines preserve the intent of the original Chinese wording:

> 让画面大胆，让语言克制。  
> 让动画惊艳，让物理解释准确。

**What this contributed:** a separation between originality of scientific discovery and creativity in explanation. It also changed the production route rather than requesting a shorter export of the old film.

## 3. Give the camera a physical job

**Source:** the same director brief.

> Start from a flat transistor schematic. Let its structures gain depth and thickness. Move around the device, enter the interface, reduce the scale, and reveal the spatial relationship of ions, charges and the surface.

> The viewer should feel that this is one physical world, explored more deeply by the camera. Spatial continuity matters more than refusing every possible cut.

**What this contributed:** an organizing visual constraint. The scene uses one device and camera function, a flat-to-spatial transformation and a magnified interfacial representation. This does not mean every structure remains a literal, scale-faithful model throughout the transition.

## 4. Stop narration from owning the timeline

**Source:** the same director brief.

> Aim for roughly one to three minutes. Do not slow down the animation to reach a fixed duration. I would rather remove Chinese narration than let it dictate the entire rhythm.

> Use concise dynamic Chinese text, background music and sound effects. Sound should support field formation, charge gathering, the move into three dimensions and the final comparison, rather than add a chime to every appearance.

**What this contributed:** no narration, an event-based synthesized soundtrack and a review of reading time. In the historical v5 edition, caption-reading intervals expanded the 102 s story coordinate to a 146.1 s film. The current English v6 edit runs for 85 s.

## 5. Turn an impression into an actionable revision

**Source:** the contributor's later visual review.

> The macro view of the ions is crowded. The upper and lower regions have similar widths, so they overlap visually. Try removing about the front third of the upper region while leaving the channel unchanged.

> A large grid at the interface could act like a floor or spatial reference and help separate the regions. Both ideas could be used together.

> Reduce the plate's strong glare, reconsider the ion colors, and fill the gap in the blue channel particles. Make the breakdown illustration more forceful. The later camera movement and text could also be more concise and energetic.

**What this contributed:** geometry, material, color and timing changes. The v5 cutaway and grid show a review comment translating into a visual solution. [Production notes](production-notes.md#feedback-that-changed-the-film) describe the responses without implying that the image is a physical simulation.

## Using these examples for another topic

Start with a short scientific chain and the errors the film must avoid. Give the animation one organizing visual idea. After inspecting a draft, point to an exact scene and explain what is unclear: which region overlaps, which sign is ambiguous, which caption is too long, or which transition loses the physical object. Preserve room for the production tool to choose the implementation.

These excerpts record this collaboration. They do not guarantee that repeating the same words with a particular model will reproduce the film.
